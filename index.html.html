Steam trap monitoring · User manual reference

How a steam trap gets its status

Every trap carries two temperature sensors, one just before it (inlet) and one just after it (outlet). Each new pair of readings is compared with limits worked out from that trap's own baseline temperatures, and the first rule that matches decides the status.

    Step 1

    Baselines become limits

    Each trap is set up with a baseline inlet temperature (what the inlet reads when steam is present, close to the steam temperature at line pressure) and a baseline outlet temperature (what the outlet reads when the trap is working properly). Most limits are a fixed fraction of a baseline. A few are fixed temperatures. Change the baselines below and every limit on this page, including the demo above, updates.

    Example values: header trap, 1.3 MPa inlet, discharge to drain
    Step 2

    Rules are checked in a fixed order

    The readings are tested against each question in turn. The first yes sets the status and the remaining questions are skipped. That order is why the map has no overlapping areas: every point on it belongs to exactly one status.

    Step 3

    Hysteresis stops the status from flickering

    Readings move a degree or two all the time. If a trap sits right on a limit, the status could switch back and forth with every reading. To prevent that, each minimum limit has a hold band that runs from 4 °C below the limit up to the limit. When a reading lands in the hold band of the trap's previous status, and that status's other limits are still met, the previous status is kept. Maximum limits have no hold band. The hold is applied to the limit, never to the baseline. On the map the hold bands are the striped strips.

    Illustration: inlet hovering around the Normal inlet limit
    Walkthrough 1: inlet cooling and warming (outlet held at 112 °C)

    Leaving Normal by cooling has a 4 °C delay. Leaving Flooding by warming has none, because 152.8 °C is a maximum limit for Flooding.

    Walkthrough 2: outlet rising and falling (inlet held at 176 °C)

    Going from Normal to Heavy leak is immediate, because 130 °C is a maximum limit for Normal. Going back to Normal needs the outlet to fall below 126 °C.

    Reference

    Status codes and what to do

    Good to know

    • Every trap has its own baselines, so two traps with the same readings can get different statuses.
    • If a baseline is changed, for example after maintenance or a change in line pressure, every limit for that trap moves with it.
    • The status is worked out again for every new reading. The dashboard shows the latest one; trend views show the history.
    • A short status change can be normal. Look for a status that holds over several readings before acting.

    What temperature alone cannot tell

    • Where the trap discharges into a pressurised condensate line, a healthy outlet already runs hot, so a small leak may change the outlet only a little.
    • A sensor clamped very close to the trap body can read warmer than the pipe further down.
    • For a trap flagged as leaking, an ultrasonic or visual check on site confirms the fault before the trap is replaced.
    The example limits use baseline inlet 191 °C and baseline outlet 100 °C. All charts on this page use illustrative data, not readings from a specific site.
